Standard Operating Procedure

The 4-Stage Doylestown Lateral Clearing Protocol

01

Cleanout Location & Flow Check

Locating basement access or perimeter riser caps. We map pipe pitch, verify whether the line discharges to municipal gravity mains or private septic headers, and evaluate drop grades.

02

Nozzle Selection & Insertion

Selecting specialized heads: penetrating chiseled nozzles for total blockages or rotary spinners for field roots and greasy septic residue. The hose enters without heavy equipment on lawns.

03

High-Volume Water Blast

Engaging up to 3,800 PSI at 18 GPM. The self-propelling rear jets push the hose deep into the line, stripping scaled buildup, shearing roots, and evacuating clay silt accumulations.

04

Reverse Flushing & Verification

Slow retraction under full rear pressure pulls detached debris and heavy solids back to collection points, ensuring an unhindered hydraulic flow through the entire run.

Local Technical FAQs

Doylestown & Chippewa Drainage Inquiries

Can hydro jetting damage our septic tank or intake baffle? expand_more
No. Hydro jetting on septic tie-ins is performed from an exterior cleanout directing water pressure downstream or upstream as needed. Technicians monitor nozzle depth precisely to stop short of damaging structural baffles or dislodging tank components.
Why do snakes keep failing on long rural driveways along Route 585? expand_more
Mechanical cable snakes depend on physical rotation driven from the machine motor. Once a cable extends beyond 75 to 100 feet, the torque drops drastically and the steel spirals into coils against the pipe walls instead of cutting obstructions. High-pressure jet hoses self-feed using hydraulic propulsion, reaching up to 350 feet with zero loss of cutting force.
Does high water pressure clear heavy agricultural clay silt from foundation footers? expand_more
Yes. Mechanical blades simply push through dense silt and leave it behind. A high-flow hydro jet delivering 16 to 18 gallons per minute fluidizes compacted clay, mud, and iron ochre into a slurry and carries it completely out of the pipe to the drainage outlet.
Is hydro jetting effective on older clay tile joints common in historic village homes? expand_more
Yes. Unlike vibrating mechanical augers that can crack brittle clay hubs, hydro jetting utilizes non-impact water volume. The stream slices tree roots clean at the joint line without subjecting the pipe structure to violent mechanical shock.
